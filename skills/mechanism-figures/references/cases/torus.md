# Grid-cell topology: Quotient out repetition; expose the underlying organization

Gardner et al. · Nature 2022 · Figure 2a–b

[Publication](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04268-7) · [Original asset](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-021-04268-7/MediaObjects/41586_2021_4268_Fig2_HTML.png)

**Use when:** Periodic or symmetric repetitions obscure a simpler state representation.

## Look in this order

1. In panel a, follow how opposite edges of the unwrapped domain correspond on the torus.
2. In b, compare repeated firing fields in physical space with compact toroidal hotspots.
3. The same activity is organized differently because the coordinates identify periodic states.

## Mechanism → construction → immediate insight

**Mechanism:** Grid-cell population activity has two periodic coordinates; physical positions can revisit the same population phase.

**Construction:** Represent periodicity on a torus and its unwrapped domain. Show physical and toroidal firing maps side by side.

**The eye sees:** The scattered repetitions are expressions of one compact periodic organization—not unrelated peaks.

**Why not an ordinary plot:** A physical heatmap shows repetition. The new coordinates reveal the shared structure behind it.

## Recreate the explanatory operation

**Replace the objects:** Grid phase → your periodic state; physical repetitions → equivalent instances; toroidal coordinates → justified identified variables.

**Preserve:** State exactly which positions are identified. Keep inferred state topology distinct from physical anatomy.

1. Show the complicated representation alongside the transformed one.
2. Make the edge identifications or symmetry action explicit.
3. Track a single state's identity across the two representations.

**Acceptance test:** Does the coordinate change preserve the relation claimed? Support topology with analysis, not the appearance of a projection.

**Do not copy literally:** A torus-shaped rendering does not establish toroidal topology.

**Transfer example (proposal, not a finding):** For periodic dynamics, pair a repeated time trace with phase coordinates and show which times map to the same phase.

**Scientific boundary:** This is a topology of population activity, not an anatomical doughnut. It does not by itself prove a specific biophysical circuit.
