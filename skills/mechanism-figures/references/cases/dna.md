**Use when:** A repeated local interface, angle or packing rule determines a global structure.

## See the mechanism

1. Look at the V-shaped module pulled out below the ring.
2. Follow its mating faces back into the assembly.
3. Compare the designed ring with the neighboring micrograph of actual assemblies.

**Mechanism:** Local building-block geometry and complementary interfaces constrain the size of a self-limiting assembly.

**Visual construction:** Pull one wedge out of the ring. Expose its interfaces. Place the corresponding experimental assemblies beside it.

**What the eye understands:** Repeating the same turn closes a loop: global size is built into the local part.

**Why an ordinary plot is weaker:** A ring-size histogram reports the outcome. The exploded assembly explains why that outcome is geometrically plausible.

## Learn this visual style, then adapt it

**Observe:** One V-shaped unit is pulled out of a closing ring and shown from useful orientations; the neighboring micrograph checks the assembled object.

**Apply:** Use an exploded view only to expose the decisive interface. Keep the extracted unit traceable to the whole and put independent physical evidence beside the predicted structure.

Record `composition_observation`, `encoding_observation`, `style_observation`, and `planned_application` for this image. Specify visible layout, persistent geometry, selective emphasis, annotation placement, color roles and whitespace—not just “clean” or “beautiful”.

## Transfer into the project

**Replace the objects:** Wedge → your repeating unit; interface → interaction rule; ring → emergent global structure; micrograph → independent observation.

**Keep the relationship:** Preserve the same module and interface in the exploded and assembled views. Show design and observation as different evidence types.

1. Show the global structure, then extract one decisive local unit without losing its location.
2. Expose the interface or geometric restriction that controls repetition.
3. Place a matched real outcome beside the predicted structure.

**Acceptance test:** Can the reader predict the global change caused by changing the local unit? Test that prediction independently.

**Do not copy literally:** An exploded view of arbitrary parts is not explanatory unless the decisive interaction is exposed.

**Scientific boundary:** The rendering explains the design. Microscopy supports assembly; geometry alone does not establish yield or defect rates.
