## See the mechanism

1. Read the antibiotic bands from the outside toward the center.
2. Follow a lineage as the colony expands and branches across those bands.
3. Keep the ancestry overlay and the photographed growth landscape in view together.

**Mechanism:** Adaptation unfolds while expanding populations encounter successively stronger antibiotic barriers.

**Visual construction:** Place the ancestry tree directly on the physical growth landscape, with resistance measurements and concentration bands.

**What the eye understands:** Evolution has a route through space: branching and advancement occur in a structured environment, not in an abstract tree.

**Why an ordinary plot is weaker:** A resistance-versus-time curve removes the geography. A detached tree removes the selective obstacles.

## Learn this visual style, then adapt it

**Observe:** Lineages are drawn directly on the colony photograph and concentration bands, retaining the geography in which adaptation occurred.

**Apply:** Let the environment carry the layout. Place histories and local measurements at their actual coordinates and keep the visual status of photographed evidence distinct from inferred ancestry.

## Transfer into the project

**Replace the objects:** Plate → your environment; concentration band → barrier; lineage → inferred history; colony image → observed state.

**Keep the relationship:** Do not detach the path from its physical coordinates. Mark inferred ancestry separately from direct observation.

1. Use the environment as the base layer.
2. Place events and trajectories at their actual locations and stages.
3. Attach the relevant local measurements without replacing the environmental context.

**Acceptance test:** Can the reader explain where progression stalled and where it advanced? Validate event ordering and ancestry separately.

**Do not copy literally:** A shortest path drawn after the fact is not an observed history.

**Scientific boundary:** The photograph is observed; ancestry connections are inferred from video. Resistance measurements and environmental concentration are distinct quantities.
