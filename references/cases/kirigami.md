## Mechanism

The cut boundary constrains deformation, guiding a stretched sheet toward different out-of-plane morphologies.

## Where to look

1. Read each flat cut boundary on the left.
2. Follow its row to the stretched three-dimensional object.
3. Compare outward, straight and inward boundaries with the different curvature types.

## Why it works

Each flat precursor is aligned with its resulting 3D shape. The boundary geometries themselves—not just numeric parameter labels—organize the alternatives.

## Take this

If geometry is the intervention, align the input geometry with its resulting deformation rather than hiding it in parameter labels.

## Test your figure

Can the reader predict the response type from the geometry? Separate changed geometry from changed loading in validation.

## Do not copy

Do not call unmatched loading conditions a controlled counterfactual.

## Limit

The shown strains differ: 0.30, 0.65 and 1.47. These are not same-strain counterfactuals, nor a universal rule for every cut pattern.
