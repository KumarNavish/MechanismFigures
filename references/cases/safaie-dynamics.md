## Mechanism

Each animal records different neurons, so the low-dimensional dynamics lie in a different plane of its neural space. A linear alignment brings the two planes together, and the trajectories then match.

## Where to look

1. Panel b: the neural landscape, with one trajectory on a surface in neural space.
2. Panel c: monkey A and monkey B do the same behaviour. Each has its own plane, blue or green, with a similar curve on it.
3. Bottom: after alignment, the two planes and the two curves lie on each other.

## Why it works

A plane in a 3D axis frame stands for a low-dimensional manifold. The same curve on two tilted planes says 'same dynamics, different coordinates' with no equation. The final overlay is the proof.
